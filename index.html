WildWatch

A radar and LoRa alert system for animal-crossing corridors on forest roads. A radar module picks up an animal near the road edge, LoRa carries that alert to a roadside signal before the animal is in a driver's line of sight, and every event is logged to a cloud dashboard.

Terra Carta Design Lab — 2024 Global Award Grant-funded by the Sustainable Markets Initiative Incubated at NID Bengaluru Field-tested at IISc Bengaluru

Overview

01

Vehicle-animal collisions on highways running through forest and wildlife corridors are, in most cases, a reaction-time problem rather than a detection problem — by the time a driver sees an animal, there often isn't enough road left to stop. WildWatch addresses this directly: a radar and LoRa alert chain that senses movement near the road edge and pushes an early warning to drivers before the animal is in their line of sight. The same detection loop doubles as a field-boundary alert for agricultural land bordering forest corridors.

The project began as a Terra Carta Design Lab entry from the National Institute of Design, Ahmedabad, and has since moved from a validated proof of concept into iterative hardware development, incubated out of NID's Bengaluru campus and built in collaboration with IISc Bengaluru. This page is the single working record of that development: what phase the project is in, what's shipped, the real field numbers, and the specific engineering calls made along the way.

Early on, the team weighed a camera-and-AI approach against a simpler radar-based one, and chose radar and LoRa for being far more tractable to build and field-harden quickly. Lab access at IISc Bengaluru, secured through Dr. G. R. Jayanth, made it possible to test the idea properly rather than on a bench alone — the first working field test recorded a 200m detection-to-alert range on campus in March 2026.

System architecture

02

The system runs as a sender/receiver pair either side of a stretch of road. Everything downstream — mount design, sensor choices, connectivity — is built around keeping this chain fast and reliable in field conditions, not around a lab bench.

Detection triggers a roadside LED alert for approaching drivers and logs the event to a cloud dashboard, giving a record of crossing frequency and location over time — useful both for tuning sensor placement and for eventually making the case for where corridors need permanent signage.

Sensor evaluation. The detection sensor has moved through several candidates rather than staying fixed: an early RCWL-0516 (5–7m range) proved the concept, then a HaiWang HW-HB100 (10–30m) carried Prototype v1 through the IISc field test. Phase 2 is evaluating two further candidates side by side — an HFS-DC06 and a DFRobot C4001 mmWave sensor — for reliable range without the RF self-interference the earlier setup ran into.

Data pipeline. Detections flow over MQTT (TLS) to a cloud broker, into a Firebase Realtime Database, and out to a live web dashboard with CSV export. Firmware applies a 500ms confirmation filter and a 60-second cooldown to cut down false triggers and repeat alerts, with automatic reconnection for WiFi and MQTT so a dropped link doesn't lose events.

A note on numbers: early technical documentation cited LoRa's spec-sheet range (multi-kilometre, open-field). The number that actually matters is the one measured in the field at IISc — about 200m at 433MHz — and that's the figure used everywhere on this page. The 868MHz migration is aimed at closing that gap between spec and reality, not just extending it further.

Funding & milestones

05
2024
Terra Carta Design Lab — Global Award

WildWatch was selected as a Global Award winner in the Terra Carta Design Lab, an initiative launched by King Charles III and Sir Jony Ive with the Royal College of Art, recognising design and technology that address the climate and biodiversity crisis. The award is funded by the Sustainable Markets Initiative, released against progress milestones.
